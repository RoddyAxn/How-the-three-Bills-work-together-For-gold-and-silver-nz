One Tick Independent

Three-Bill Parliamentary Package

Monetary Sovereignty • Property Rights • Strategic Asset Protection

Roderick J. Young — 4 September 2026

This package is designed around a simple division of responsibility: Parliament makes clear rules, the Executive administers them, and the courts determine disputed rights and equitable remedies.
WORK — SAVE — OWN — PROTECT

BILL 1

Monetary Sovereignty and Precious Metal Savings Bill

Purpose: To create voluntary gold and silver savings, a sovereign bullion reserve, transparent mineral-resource accounting, and a lawful separation between fully allocated precious-metal savings and ordinary fiat-credit banking.

1 Title
This Act is the Monetary Sovereignty and Precious Metal Savings Act 2027.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force 12 months after Royal assent.
3 Purposes
The purposes are to increase monetary choice, permit precious-metal-denominated savings, establish a New Zealand Gold and Silver Bank, protect fully allocated bullion accounts, create a Sovereign Bullion Reserve, improve mineral-resource transparency, reduce unnecessary dependence on offshore credit, and preserve the lawful use of the New Zealand dollar and commercial banking.
4 Principles
Decision makers must have regard to monetary stability, freedom of contract, protection of lawful savings, transparency, prudential soundness, intergenerational responsibility, and New Zealand's international obligations.

Part 2 — New Zealand Gold and Silver Bank

5 Bank established
A Crown entity called the New Zealand Gold and Silver Bank — Te Pēke Koura me te Hiriwa o Aotearoa is established.
6 Functions
The Bank may acquire and hold investment-grade gold and silver; operate allocated bullion accounts; facilitate transfers; buy and sell precious metals; provide secure storage; issue ownership statements by weight and fineness; and exchange bullion units with New Zealand dollars.
7 Gold Unit
A Gold Unit is a prescribed weight of fine gold.
8 Silver Unit
A Silver Unit is a prescribed weight of fine silver.
9 Fully allocated bullion
Bullion backing a fully allocated account must be separately recorded, held for the relevant account holder, and not pledged, lent, or treated as unsecured bank property except under express lawful authority and informed consent.
10 One-hundred-percent backing
The total quantity of Gold Units and Silver Units represented as fully allocated must not exceed the corresponding qualifying bullion actually held.
11 Audit
Holdings must be audited annually and physically verified at least every 6 months.
12 Public reporting
The Bank must publish audited totals for units outstanding, bullion holdings, fineness standards, liabilities, and material reserve movements.

Part 3 — Relationship with ordinary currency

13 New Zealand dollar unaffected
Nothing in this Act abolishes or diminishes the lawful status of New Zealand currency.
14 Voluntary acceptance
No person is required to accept a Gold Unit or Silver Unit unless that person agrees.
15 Contracts
Parties may lawfully measure obligations in New Zealand dollars, prescribed weights of gold or silver, or a lawful combination.
16 Taxes
Unless another enactment expressly provides otherwise, taxes remain assessed and payable in New Zealand dollars.

Part 4 — Sovereign Bullion Reserve

17 Reserve established
A New Zealand Sovereign Bullion Reserve is established as Crown property.
18 Funding
Parliament may appropriate money to the Reserve from mineral royalties, Crown mining income, budget surpluses, or other lawful sources.
19 No automatic monetisation
Unextracted minerals must not be treated as cash or immediately available revenue solely by reference to gross estimated commodity value.
20 National Mineral Resource Account
An independently prepared account must be published at least once every 3 years, distinguishing geological resources, identified resources, economically recoverable reserves, Crown and private rights, extraction and rehabilitation costs, and estimated net Crown revenue.
21 Disposal of sovereign bullion
Material disposal, pledge, or export of sovereign bullion requires statutory authority or an express parliamentary appropriation.

Part 5 — Border treatment and review

22 Importation of private bullion
Lawfully acquired gold and silver may enter New Zealand subject to customs, provenance, anti-money-laundering, and declaration requirements.
23 Private bullion protected
Nothing in this Act authorises arbitrary confiscation of lawfully owned private bullion.
24 Regulations
Regulations may prescribe standards for fineness, custody, auditing, account disclosure, and transaction reporting.
25 Five-year review
A select committee must review the Act no later than 5 years after commencement.

BILL 2

Property Rights and Just Compensation Bill

Purpose: To create an express statutory presumption in favour of lawful property rights, require clear authority for compulsory acquisition or substantial deprivation, and provide a just-compensation framework.

1 Title
This Act is the Property Rights and Just Compensation Act 2027.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force 6 months after Royal assent.
3 Purpose
The purpose is to strengthen respect for lawful property rights while preserving Parliament's ability to regulate in the public interest.
4 Interpretation
Property includes real and personal property, money, securities, contractual rights, intellectual property, and legally recognised proprietary interests. Public authority includes the Crown, Ministers, departments, Crown entities, local authorities, and statutory bodies. Substantial deprivation means State action that removes substantially all practical economic use or value of a property right.

Part 2 — General property-rights guarantee

5 Peaceful enjoyment
Every person is entitled, subject to law, to the peaceful enjoyment, use, transfer, saving, investment, and disposition of lawful property.
6 No taking without clear authority
No public authority may compulsorily acquire or substantially deprive a person of property except under clear statutory authority.
7 Public purpose
A compulsory acquisition must be for a public purpose authorised by law.
8 Proportionality
A material interference with property must use means reasonably proportionate to the lawful public purpose.
9 Lesser interference preferred
Where two reasonably practicable measures achieve substantially the same public purpose, the authority should prefer the measure causing the lesser unjustified interference with private property.

Part 3 — Just compensation

10 Right to just compensation
Where property is compulsorily acquired, the owner is entitled to just compensation unless Parliament expressly provides otherwise in unmistakable terms.
11 Basis of compensation
Compensation should, so far as money reasonably can, place the owner in the position the owner would have occupied had the compulsory acquisition not occurred.
12 Components
Compensation may include market value, reasonable relocation costs, professional and valuation expenses, direct severance or disturbance loss, reasonable financing consequences, and interest from taking until payment.
13 No windfall
Speculative, remote, or double-counted loss is not compensable.
14 Offer before possession
Except in a lawful emergency, the public authority must make a written compensation offer before taking possession or control.

Part 4 — Regulatory deprivation

15 Ordinary regulation
A lawful regulation that merely limits property use does not automatically create a right to compensation.
16 Substantial deprivation
If regulatory action removes substantially all practical economic use or value, the High Court may determine whether justice requires compensation.
17 Matters for Court
The Court may consider the nature of the right, extent and duration of interference, reasonable investment-backed expectations, whether the measure is general or targeted, the public interest, claimant conduct, alternative lawful uses, and any mitigation already provided.

Part 5 — High Court and remedies

18 High Court jurisdiction
The High Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine proceedings under this Act.
19 Remedies
The Court may grant declarations, injunctions, compensation, restitution, interest, costs, or any remedy available according to law or equity.
20 Equity preserved
Nothing in this Act limits the High Court's equitable jurisdiction.
21 Judicial review preserved
Nothing in this Act limits the right to apply for judicial review.

Part 6 — Legislative discipline

22 Property-rights statement
A Minister introducing a Government Bill that authorises compulsory acquisition or material interference with property must identify the property interests affected, public purpose, compensation position, proportionality, and less-restrictive alternatives considered.
23 Express override required
No later enactment should be interpreted as excluding compensation under this Act unless it does so expressly or by necessary implication.
24 Emergency powers
Temporary emergency measures remain available where authorised by law, but any uncompensated taking must be reviewed as soon as reasonably practicable after the emergency.
25 Existing rights preserved
This Act supplements, and does not displace, any greater compensation entitlement under another enactment.
26 Five-year review
A select committee must review the Act no later than 5 years after commencement.

BILL 3

Strategic New Zealand Assets and Predatory Foreign Credit Bill

Purpose: To distinguish productive foreign investment from debt-funded acquisition of strategic existing assets, strengthen beneficial-ownership transparency, and provide remedies against predatory or unconscionable foreign credit.

1 Title
This Act is the Strategic New Zealand Assets and Predatory Foreign Credit Act 2027.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force 12 months after Royal assent.
3 Purposes
The purposes are to protect strategic New Zealand assets from unacceptable debt-related transfer risk; encourage productive foreign investment; increase beneficial-ownership and funding transparency; prevent predatory foreign lending; protect competition and national resilience; and ensure restrictions are exercised transparently and according to law.
4 Interpretation
Foreign credit means credit provided or substantially funded from outside New Zealand. Strategic asset means an asset designated for its significance to national security, resilience, infrastructure, natural resources, food production, housing stability, finance, communications, or essential services. Ultimate beneficial owner means the natural person or persons who ultimately own, control, or economically benefit from an entity or arrangement.

Part 2 — Strategic assets

5 Strategic asset classes
Classes may include ports and airports; electricity infrastructure; water infrastructure; telecommunications and data infrastructure; major banking and payment infrastructure; strategic mineral rights and processing facilities; significant agricultural and forestry land; nationally significant housing portfolios; fuel-import, storage, refining, and distribution infrastructure; and rail, freight, and logistics infrastructure.
6 No blanket prohibition
Foreign ownership or finance is not prohibited merely because it is foreign.
7 Productive investment principle
Decision makers must distinguish investment that creates productive capacity, jobs, technology, infrastructure, or exports from highly leveraged acquisition of existing strategic assets where the principal effect is ownership transfer and increased debt exposure.

Part 3 — Approval of debt-funded strategic acquisitions

8 Additional approval
A designated strategic acquisition funded above a prescribed level by foreign credit requires approval under this Act in addition to any other legal approval.
9 Assessment criteria
The decision maker must consider leverage, refinancing risk, currency risk, ultimate beneficial ownership, source of funds, national security, resilience, competition, employment, productive-capacity creation, forced-sale risk, tax-base effects, environmental liabilities, and international obligations.
10 Conditions
Approval may impose conditions concerning minimum equity, maximum leverage, local governance, asset maintenance, disclosure, refinancing, and continuity of essential services.
11 Reasons
Approval or refusal must state material reasons, subject to lawful protection of commercially sensitive or national-security information.

Part 4 — Beneficial ownership and funding transparency

12 Beneficial ownership disclosure
An applicant must disclose ultimate beneficial owners, controlling interests, material funding sources, related-party lenders, and guarantees.
13 False or misleading information
Knowingly or recklessly providing materially false or misleading information is an offence and may attract civil penalties.
14 Ongoing disclosure
A material change in beneficial ownership, control, or funding must be notified within the prescribed period.

Part 5 — Predatory foreign credit

15 Predatory foreign credit prohibited
A lender must not enter into or enforce a foreign-credit arrangement that is unconscionable, fraudulent, materially deceptive, or structured principally to defeat New Zealand law.
16 Indicators
Relevant indicators include concealed effective interest or charges; grossly disproportionate default penalties; excessive security; manipulated valuations; undisclosed related parties; concealed refinancing risk; exploitation of severe bargaining inequality; evasion of New Zealand law; concealment of beneficial ownership; and unreasonable forced-transfer risk for strategic assets.
17 Legitimate commercial risk preserved
A loan is not predatory merely because it is profitable, secured, unsuccessful, or enforced after genuine default, provided it was lawful, transparent, and not unconscionable.

Part 6 — Court powers

18 High Court jurisdiction
The High Court has jurisdiction under this Act.
19 Remedies
The Court may reopen a transaction; vary interest or fees; modify or set aside security; restrain enforcement; order restitution or repayment; grant an injunction; award damages; impose authorised civil penalties; or make another order available according to law or equity.
20 Innocent third parties
The Court must consider the rights of innocent third parties who acquired interests for value and without notice of wrongdoing.

Part 7 — National resilience

21 Continuity of essential services
If enforcement against a strategic asset would materially threaten an essential service, the responsible Minister may apply to the High Court for a temporary continuity order.
22 Continuity order
The order may temporarily restrain disposal or enforcement while arrangements are made to preserve essential services, but only for the period reasonably necessary.
23 Compensation
If a continuity order causes compensable loss beyond ordinary commercial risk, the Court may award compensation on just terms.

Part 8 — Parliamentary accountability

24 Annual report
The responsible Minister must report annually to Parliament on strategic acquisitions, approvals, conditions, enforcement actions, and systemic risks.
25 Public register
A public register of approved strategic acquisitions must be maintained, subject to lawful redactions.
26 Regulations
Regulations may prescribe strategic-asset thresholds, leverage thresholds, disclosure requirements, filing procedures, and civil-penalty levels.
27 Relationship with other enactments
This Act supplements overseas-investment, companies, financial-markets, credit, anti-money-laundering, competition, insolvency, tax, and national-security law.
28 Five-year review
A select committee must review the Act no later than 5 years after commencement.

How the three Bills work together

Bill 1 creates monetary choice and a protected domestic savings mechanism.

Bill 2 establishes property-rights and compensation principles governing State interference with ownership.

Bill 3 addresses the point where foreign credit, strategic assets, national resilience, and predatory lending intersect.

The package does not seek to close New Zealand to trade, tourism, banking, or foreign investment. It seeks stronger boundaries between saving and credit creation, regulation and confiscation, and productive foreign capital and predatory acquisition.
